## Overview
A reliable 5G service for emergency responders requires cooperation across six IT fields. Software engineering ensures that applications handle failed requests and retries without causing unintended actions. Artificial intelligence helps predict network congestion, allowing operators to prepare before performance declines. Mobile development enables responders to continue essential work during connectivity loss and synchronize information when service returns. Networking provides the infrastructure and traffic management needed to prioritize emergency communications. Cybersecurity protects the service by preparing for attacks, detecting threats, and restoring affected systems. Data engineering organizes network usage records into meaningful reports by region and day, supporting operational decisions. Together, these fields improve service availability, resilience, and accountability during emergencies. Their combined efforts help responders communicate effectively when network conditions become unpredictable.
## Software
HTTP safe methods are GET, HEAD, OPTIONS, and TRACE because they are intended only for read-only operations. Idempotent methods include all safe methods, plus PUT and DELETE, because repeating them has the same intended effect as sending them once. POST is generally neither safe nor idempotent. When a request fails, automatic retries of idempotent methods reduce the risk of unintended duplicate operations, which is important for emergency service reliability.
